Circuit Documentation

1. Circuit Summary

This circuit is a battery-powered, four-wheel motor platform controlled by an ESP32-C3 Super Mini. Two L9110 dual-channel motor-driver modules control four motors:

  • One L9110 module controls the front-left and back-left motors.
  • The other L9110 module controls the front-right and back-right motors.
  • The ESP32-C3 provides four digital/PWM control signals to the two motor drivers.
  • Two 18650 Li-ion cells are connected in parallel to the battery terminals of a USB power-bank module.
  • The power-bank module provides a nominal 5 V output to the ESP32-C3 and both L9110 modules.
  • All controller and motor-driver grounds are connected to the power-bank output ground.

No microcontroller firmware was provided with the circuit.

Important design note: The ESP32-C3 pin is labeled 5V:OUTPUT, but it is connected to the power-bank module’s Out + 5V output. Electrically, this connection treats the ESP32 pin as a 5 V supply input. Verify the actual board pin function before powering the circuit. Do not connect two power outputs together.

3. Component List

3.1 ESP32-C3 Super Mini

Microcontroller used to control the two L9110 motor-driver modules. Four GPIO pins are connected to the motor-driver input pins and may be driven with digital signals or PWM for speed control.

Connected pins:

  • 5 V power connection
  • Ground
  • GPIO 3 / A3
  • GPIO 4 / A4 / SCK
  • GPIO 5 / A5 / MISO
  • GPIO 6 / MOSI
  • GPIO 7 / SS
  • GPIO 10
  • GPIO 0 / A0
  • GPIO 1 / A1

Only eight GPIO/control connections are electrically used in the provided net list; four of these drive the two motor-driver modules.

3.2 USB Power-Bank Module

Provides battery charging/power-management functionality and a regulated 5 V output.

Connections:

  • Batt + connects to the positive terminals of both 18650 batteries.
  • Batt - connects to the negative terminals of both 18650 batteries.
  • Out + 5V powers the ESP32-C3 and both L9110 modules.
  • out - 5V provides the common ground return.

The exact battery-management behavior depends on the specific power-bank module. Confirm that the module supports parallel-connected 18650 cells and includes appropriate protection.

3.3 18650 Li-ion Battery

First 18650 cell. It is connected in parallel with the second 18650 cell.

Connections:

  • Positive terminal to the power-bank module’s Batt +.
  • Negative terminal to the power-bank module’s Batt -.

3.4 18650 Li-ion Battery (Second Cell)

Second 18650 cell. It is connected in parallel with the first 18650 cell.

Connections:

  • Positive terminal to the power-bank module’s Batt +.
  • Negative terminal to the power-bank module’s Batt -.

The two cells must be compatible and suitably matched before being connected in parallel. Ideally, they should be the same chemistry, capacity, age, and state of charge.

3.5 L9110 Motor Driver — Left-Side Motors

Dual-channel motor driver used for the front-left and back-left motors.

Connections:

  • VCC to regulated 5 V.
  • GND to power-bank output ground.
  • Channel A controls the front-left motor.
  • Channel B controls the back-left motor.
  • A1 and A2 receive control signals from the ESP32-C3.
  • B1 and B2 receive control signals from the ESP32-C3.
  • Motor outputs connect to the two left-side motors.

3.6 L9110 Motor Driver — Right-Side Motors

Dual-channel motor driver used for the front-right and back-right motors.

Connections:

  • VCC to regulated 5 V.
  • GND to power-bank output ground.
  • Channel A controls the back-right motor.
  • Channel B controls the front-right motor.
  • A1 and A2 receive control signals from the ESP32-C3.
  • B1 and B2 receive control signals from the ESP32-C3.
  • Motor outputs connect to the two right-side motors.

3.7 FRONT_LEFT Motor and Wheels

DC motor and associated wheel assembly.

Connections:

  • Motor vcc connects to the first L9110 module’s MA1.
  • Motor GND connects to the first L9110 module’s MA2.

The motor direction depends on the polarity produced by the L9110 channel and the physical motor wiring.

3.8 FRONT_RIGHT Motor and Wheels

DC motor and associated wheel assembly.

Connections:

  • Motor vcc connects to the second L9110 module’s M1B.
  • Motor GND connects to the second L9110 module’s MB2.

3.9 BACK_LEFT Motor and Wheels

DC motor and associated wheel assembly.

Connections:

  • Motor vcc connects to the first L9110 module’s M1B.
  • Motor GND connects to the first L9110 module’s MB2.

3.10 BACK_RIGHT Motor and Wheels

DC motor and associated wheel assembly.

Connections:

  • Motor vcc connects to the second L9110 module’s MA1.
  • Motor GND connects to the second L9110 module’s MA2.

5. Power and Ground Architecture

The power flow is:

18650 batteries in parallel
         |
         v
USB power-bank module
         |
         +---- Regulated 5 V ---- ESP32-C3
         |
         +---- Regulated 5 V ---- Left-side L9110
         |
         +---- Regulated 5 V ---- Right-side L9110

The ground flow is:

Power-bank output ground
         |
         +---- ESP32-C3 GND
         |
         +---- Left-side L9110 GND
         |
         +---- Right-side L9110 GND

The motors are powered through the L9110 output stages. Their motor terminals are not connected directly to the 5 V supply.

Because motors generate electrical noise and transient current, practical construction should include:

  • Short, low-resistance motor-power wiring.
  • A bulk capacitor near the motor-driver supply pins.
  • Local ceramic bypass capacitors near each motor-driver module.
  • Physical separation between motor wiring and sensitive ESP32 wiring where possible.
  • A supply capable of handling the combined motor startup and stall current.

The power-bank module’s 5 V output must be rated for the combined current required by the ESP32, both L9110 modules, and all four motors.

6. Documented Code

No microcontroller code was provided.

The ESP32-C3 firmware will need to implement at least the following functions:

  1. Configure the eight motor-control GPIO pins as outputs.
  2. Generate direction control signals for each L9110 channel.
  3. Use PWM, if supported by the selected framework and pin configuration, to control motor speed.
  4. Provide a safe stop state during startup and shutdown.
  5. Avoid invalid or unsafe combinations of the two control inputs on each motor-driver channel.
  6. Optionally compensate for the reversed physical orientation of motors on opposite sides of the vehicle.
